About

My background is in Linguistics, but I kept running into the same problem everywhere: people don't get stuck because something is hard — they get stuck because it's confusing. That's what pulled me from writing into UX and technical writing.

At Mainstack, I designed and wrote two products from scratch — Bookings and Hosted Courses — including the PRDs and every word of copy that shipped. I also redesigned Link-in-Bio around real creator personas, and rewrote Pay-in-Tranches so a fairly technical payment feature felt simple to first-time buyers. I've led content and communications for Placements and DearDesigner, and I currently teach multimodal writing at the University of Florida, where I write the syllabi and course docs students rely on with no one else to ask.

Mainstack — BookingsMainstack

Product design · UX writing · PRD — built from scratch
0→1 build Solo design + writing PRD + handoff docs
Problem

Creators had no way to sell time — consultations, calls, sessions — without juggling a calendar app, an invoicing tool, and DMs.

What I did

Designed the booking flow, wrote every screen, and authored the PRD engineering built from. No existing product to redesign — just a blank page.

Result

One flow that handles availability, payment, and confirmation across time zones, with no manual back-and-forth.

Full PRD breakdown
  1. Purpose — Replace scattered scheduling tools with one native booking-and-payment flow.
  2. Scope — v1: single-creator availability, 1:1 bookings, combined payment, automated reminders, cancellation policy. Out of scope: group bookings, calendar sync (v2).
  3. Personas — The Creator, who manages availability and needs protection from no-shows; the Client, who needs certainty on time, timezone, and payment.
  4. User flows — Creator sets availability and publishes slots; client books a slot, pays, and gets confirmation.
  5. Functional requirements — Availability calendar, automatic timezone conversion, combined checkout, confirmation/reminder notifications, cancellation flow.
  6. Acceptance criteria — e.g. a client always sees slot times converted to their own timezone, with the creator's timezone shown for reference.
  7. Edge cases — Double bookings, cancellation after payment, daylight-saving shifts, a failed payment on a held slot.
  8. Dependencies — Engineering (booking logic, payments), design (visual system), support (refund policy language).

Mainstack — Hosted CoursesMainstack

Product design · UX writing · PRD — built from scratch
0→1 build Multi-currency PRD + handoff docs
Problem

Creators were stitching together a payment processor, a video host, and a certificate tool just to run one paid course.

What I did

Designed the full creator-to-student flow and wrote the PRD and every word of copy, from course setup to certificate.

Result

One product that works the same way for creators and students anywhere, in their own currency.

Full PRD breakdown
  1. Purpose — Replace fragmented tooling with one native course platform.
  2. Scope — v1: modules/lessons, multi-currency pricing, progress tracking, certificates. Out of scope: live cohorts, multi-instructor courses (v2).
  3. Personas — The Creator, packaging expertise into a course; the Student, enrolling from anywhere, needing price in their own currency and proof of progress.
  4. User flows — Creator builds and publishes a course; student enrolls, pays, progresses through modules, and gets a certificate.
  5. Functional requirements — Drag-and-drop module ordering, currency conversion at checkout, a progress dashboard, certificate generation, creator-facing analytics.
  6. Acceptance criteria — e.g. course price is always shown converted to the student's local currency, alongside the base price.
  7. Edge cases — Refunds mid-course, partial completion, currency mismatches, a module edited after students have enrolled.
  8. Dependencies — Engineering (infrastructure, payments), design (certificate templates), support (refund policy language).

Mainstack — Pay-in-TranchesMainstack

UX writing — copy only
Microcopy audit Copy only
Problem

Pay-in-Tranches let customers split a payment into installments, but the copy leaned on financial jargon that confused first-time buyers.

What I did

Audited every screen where a customer saw payment-plan language and rewrote it around one rule: always state the amount and the date, never the mechanism.

Result

Clearer checkout copy that tells a buyer exactly what they're agreeing to, plus a short style note so future copy stays consistent.

Blood Donation AppPersonal

Product design
Personal project
Problem

Most blood donation apps get two things wrong: the emergency flow feels alarming instead of clear, and eligibility questions use clinical language that puts people off donating.

What I did

Rewrote the emergency flow to be short and direct, turned clinical eligibility questions into plain yes/no ones, and explained in one line why sensitive info was needed before asking for it.

Result

A calmer sign-up for donors, and a request flow built for people acting under real pressure.

Instructional Writing

Canvas / Syllabus Design
A syllabus is the first onboarding document a student sees. It has to work for a total stranger, with no one to ask.

As an Instructor of Record at the University of Florida, I build the Canvas course pages for ENC 1136 (and previously ENC 1102) — syllabi, assignment instructions, rubrics, and module sequencing that students rely on with no other point of contact. I treat it like product onboarding: what does someone need to know before they can act, and where will they get stuck if I don't address it in the copy itself?

The skills are the same as UX writing — audience analysis, information hierarchy, anticipating confusion before it happens. Only the interface changes.
